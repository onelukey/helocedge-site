HELOC vs. Home Equity Loan: Which Choice Is Best for You?

Published: June 2026 | Written by HELOC Edge Editorial Team

If you have built up significant equity in your home, you are sitting on a powerful financial tool. When it comes time to tap into that value—whether for home renovations, consolidating high-interest debt, or handling unexpected expenses—you will generally find yourself facing two distinct pathways: a Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) or a Home Equity Loan.

While both allow you to borrow against the appraised value of your property, their underlying mechanisms, interest structures, and payment schedules couldn't be more different. Choosing the wrong one could cost you thousands in unnecessary interest charges.

1. What is a Home Equity Loan?

Often referred to as a "second mortgage," a Home Equity Loan is a lump-sum loan. Once approved, the lender hands you the full amount upfront. From day one, you begin making fixed monthly payments that cover both the principal balance and a fixed interest rate.

The Main Advantage: Predictability. Because your interest rate is completely locked in, your payment will remain identical for the life of the loan (typically 5 to 30 years). It protects you perfectly if market interest rates spike.

2. What is a HELOC?

A HELOC operates much more like a high-limit credit card. Instead of getting a lump sum, you are approved for a maximum borrowing limit. You can withdraw as much or as little as you need during an initial timeframe known as the Draw Period (usually 10 years).

During the draw period, you typically only owe interest on the exact amount you have pulled out, making your initial payments incredibly low. Once that period ends, you enter the Repayment Period (usually 15 to 20 years), where you must pay back both the principal and interest.

The Main Advantage: Flexibility. If you are approved for $50,000 but only end up using $10,000 for a kitchen remodel, you only pay interest on that $10,000.

Quick Comparison At-A-Glance

  • Payout: Home Equity Loan is a single lump sum; HELOC is a flexible, revolving line of credit.
  • Interest Rates: Home Equity Loans feature stable, fixed rates. HELOCs naturally feature variable rates that fluctuate with the wider economy.
  • Payments: Fixed loans demand equal payments immediately. HELOCs offer cheap interest-only options early on, followed by larger amortized payments later.
Cross-Border Note: Are you a Canadian Borrower?

While the functional logic above applies universally, Canadian homeowners should note that Canadian lenders often bundle a HELOC directly with your primary mortgage (often called a Readvancable Mortgage). Additionally, under current OSFI guidelines in Canada, the revolving HELOC portion by itself can only scale up to a maximum of 65% of your home's total value, whereas US lenders may occasionally allow higher leverage limits.

The Verdict: How to Choose

If you have a large, single expense with a clear price tag (like a complete foundation repair or buying a second property), a Home Equity Loan gives you the long-term safety of a locked-in rate.

However, if you want an ongoing financial safety net, have a staggered renovation project over several years, or want variable access to cash flow, a HELOC offers unmatched flexibility.
