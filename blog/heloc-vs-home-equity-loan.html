HELOC vs. Home Equity Loan: Which Choice Is Best for You?

5 min read Mike Lucas

If you've built up significant equity in your home, you're sitting on a powerful financial tool. When it comes time to tap into that value, whether for renovations, consolidating high-interest debt, or handling unexpected expenses, you'll generally find yourself facing two distinct pathways: a Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) or a Home Equity Loan.

Both let you borrow against your home's appraised value, but their mechanisms, interest structures, and payment schedules couldn't be more different. Choosing the wrong one could cost you thousands in unnecessary interest.

What Is a Home Equity Loan?

Often called a "second mortgage," a Home Equity Loan is a lump-sum loan. Once approved, the lender hands you the full amount upfront. From day one, you make fixed monthly payments covering both principal and a fixed interest rate.

The main advantage: predictability. Because the interest rate is locked in, your payment stays identical for the life of the loan, typically 5 to 30 years, protecting you from market rate increases.

What Is a HELOC?

A HELOC works much more like a high-limit credit card. Instead of a lump sum, you're approved for a maximum borrowing limit and can withdraw as much or as little as you need during the draw period (usually 10 years).

During the draw period, you typically only owe interest on the amount you've actually drawn, keeping initial payments low. Once that period ends, you enter the repayment period (usually 15 to 20 years), where you pay back both principal and interest. Our HELOC Payment Calculator breaks down exactly what both phases would cost you.

The main advantage: flexibility. If you're approved for $50,000 but only end up using $10,000 for a kitchen remodel, you only pay interest on that $10,000.

Quick Comparison At-A-Glance

Feature HELOC Home Equity Loan
Payout Flexible, revolving line of credit Single lump sum
Interest rate Variable Fixed
Early payments Interest-only, lower Full amortizing from day one
Best suited for Ongoing or staggered expenses One large, known expense

The Verdict: How to Choose

If you have a large, single expense with a clear price tag, like a complete foundation repair or buying a second property, a Home Equity Loan gives you the long-term safety of a locked-in rate.

If you want an ongoing financial safety net, have a staggered renovation project over several years, or want variable access to cash flow, a HELOC offers unmatched flexibility. Either way, it's worth checking how much you could actually borrow first using the Borrowing Power Calculator.
