Planning a Renovation? Here's a Financing Option Most Homeowners Overlook

8 min read
Mike Lucas — Founder

If you're planning a kitchen remodel, a new roof, or finally converting the garage into a livable space, there's a good chance you've already started pricing out how to pay for it. Homeowners may compare savings, credit cards, personal loans, home equity loans and a Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC). Each option has different costs, repayment terms and risks.

A HELOC is one possible way to finance renovation expenses, but it should be compared with other borrowing and non-borrowing options. The amount available, interest rate, fees and repayment obligations depend on the lender and the homeowner's circumstances.

What a HELOC actually is

A HELOC is a revolving line of credit secured against the equity in your home — revolving in structure, but backed by your property. You may draw funds during an agreed draw period, with interest generally calculated on the outstanding balance. Required payments may include principal, interest and fees depending on the agreement, and repayment terms can change after the draw period.

Comparing renovation financing options

There are a few practical reasons a HELOC gets overlooked during renovation planning:

  • Timing pressure. A leaking roof or a broken HVAC system doesn't wait for a HELOC application to close, so homeowners reach for the card already in their wallet.
  • Unfamiliarity with the process. A HELOC application involves a home appraisal and underwriting, which can feel like more effort than a same-day personal loan approval.
  • Not realizing how much equity is available. Many homeowners underestimate how much their home has appreciated, and therefore assume they don't have meaningful equity to borrow against.
  • Confusing a HELOC with a second mortgage. The two are related but not identical — a HELOC is flexible and revolving, while a home equity loan is a fixed lump sum with fixed payments.

Different financing methods suit different needs. Comparing the full cost, timing, repayment requirements and collateral risks is more informative than comparing interest rates alone.

The real cost of defaulting to credit cards

Secured and unsecured products can carry different rates, but the rate offered to any borrower is not guaranteed. Compare annual percentage rates, fees, repayment periods and the total amount repaid. A HELOC also puts the home at risk if required payments are not made.

How a HELOC can work for renovation spending

Home improvement projects rarely involve a single, predictable expense. Costs shift as a project uncovers surprises — a contractor quote, a permit fee, an unexpected plumbing issue, a change of mind on materials halfway through. A HELOC's revolving structure may be useful to understand alongside personal loans, home equity loans and cash-out refinancing:

  • You draw only what you need, when you need it. There's no obligation to borrow the full approved line up front, and no interest accrues on the untouched portion.
  • It suits phased or multi-project renovations. If you're tackling the kitchen this year and the bathroom next year, a HELOC lets you draw again without a fresh loan application each time.
  • It leaves your first mortgage untouched. Unlike a cash-out refinance, a HELOC doesn't require you to refinance your existing mortgage — which matters a great deal if your current mortgage rate is lower than today's market rate.
The core trade-off to understand

Important risk: A HELOC is secured by your home. Missing required payments could lead to foreclosure and loss of the property. Variable rates can also increase borrowing costs and required payments. Compare repayment obligations and risks rather than relying on a borrowing-limit estimate.

Factors to consider when comparing renovation financing

Questions that may help when comparing financing options include:

  • You have meaningful equity built up in your home
  • Your renovation costs are likely to be spread out or uncertain in total
  • You want to avoid disturbing a favorable existing mortgage rate
  • You're comfortable with a variable interest rate, which is standard for most HELOCs

Limited equity, a need for predictable payments, fees, and the possibility of rising rates can all affect the comparison. No calculator or article can determine whether a borrowing product is appropriate for an individual homeowner.
