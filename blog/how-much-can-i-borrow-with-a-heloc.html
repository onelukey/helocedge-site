How Much Can I Reasonably Borrow With a HELOC?

Published: June 2026 | Written by HELOC Edge Editorial Team

When homeowners decide to open a Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC), the very first question they ask is almost always: "How much cash can I actually get out of my house?"

Many people mistakenly believe that if they have $100,000 worth of equity in their property, a bank will hand them a $100,000 line of credit. Unfortunately, underwriting rules are tighter than that. Lenders will never allow you to borrow against the entirety of your home's value.

To find your true borrowing power, you have to understand the metric that every single underwriter uses: Combined Loan-to-Value (CLTV).

1. The Golden Rule: The 80% CLTV Cap

While requirements vary slightly between institutions, the vast majority of traditional banks and credit unions cap your total combined housing debt at 80% to 85% of your home's total appraised value.

This means your primary first mortgage balance PLUS your new proposed HELOC limit combined cannot exceed that percentage threshold. The remaining 15% to 20% acts as a financial protective cushion for the bank in case real estate market prices decline.

2. How to Calculate Your Limit (Step-by-Step)

Let's run through a realistic real-world math scenario to show exactly how a bank processes your borrowing limit:

The Baseline Scenario:

  • Estimated Current Home Value: $400,000
  • Current Remaining Mortgage Balance: $250,000
  • Lender Maximum CLTV Allowed: 80%

The Calculation:

  1. Find the absolute borrowing ceiling: Multiply your home value by the CLTV cap.
    $400,000 × 0.80 = $320,000
  2. Subtract what you already owe: Deduct your current primary mortgage balance from that ceiling.
    $320,000 - $250,000 = $70,000

Maximum Available HELOC Limit: $70,000

3. Other Factors That Influence Your Final Line

Passing the home equity percentage math test is only half the battle. Lenders will also run deep checks on your individual financial stability metrics to determine if you can realistically afford to service the line of credit:

  • Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio: Lenders typically want your total recurring monthly debt obligations (mortgage, car loans, minimum credit card choices, plus the maximum potential HELOC payment) to consume less than 43% of your verifiable gross monthly income.
  • Credit Score: While you can secure a primary mortgage with lower credit metrics, HELOC approvals typically require a stronger credit history. Scoring above 720 unlocks the absolute best variable interest rate tiers.

Conclusion

Before applying for a home equity line, look at recent comparable sales in your immediate neighborhood to establish an honest appraisal value baseline, calculate your 80% cap, and check your credit status. Knowing your numbers ahead of time gives you a massive advantage when negotiating loan terms with potential lenders.
