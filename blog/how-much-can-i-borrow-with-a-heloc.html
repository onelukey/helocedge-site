How Much Can I Reasonably Borrow With a HELOC?

5 min read Mike Lucas

When homeowners decide to open a Home Equity Line of Credit, the very first question they ask is almost always: "How much cash can I actually get out of my house?"

Many people mistakenly believe that if they have $100,000 worth of equity in their property, a bank will hand them a $100,000 line of credit. Unfortunately, underwriting rules are tighter than that. Lenders will never let you borrow against the entirety of your home's value.

To find your true borrowing power, you need to understand the metric every underwriter uses: Combined Loan-to-Value (CLTV).

The Golden Rule: The 80% CLTV Cap

While requirements vary slightly between institutions, the vast majority of traditional banks and credit unions cap your total combined housing debt at 80% to 85% of your home's appraised value.

This means your primary mortgage balance plus your new proposed HELOC limit, combined, cannot exceed that percentage threshold. The remaining 15–20% acts as a financial cushion for the bank in case real estate prices decline.

How to Calculate Your Limit (Step-by-Step)

Let's run through a realistic scenario to show exactly how a bank processes your borrowing limit:

Item Amount
Estimated current home value $400,000
Current remaining mortgage balance $250,000
Lender maximum CLTV allowed 80%
Step 1: Home value × CLTV cap ($400,000 × 0.80) $320,000
Step 2: Subtract existing mortgage ($320,000 − $250,000) $70,000

Maximum available HELOC limit in this example: $70,000

Other Factors That Influence Your Final Line

Passing the equity percentage test is only half the battle. Lenders also run deep checks on your individual financial stability to determine if you can realistically afford to service the line of credit:

  • Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio: Lenders typically want your total recurring monthly debt (mortgage, car loans, minimum credit card payments, plus the maximum potential HELOC payment) to consume less than 43% of your verifiable gross monthly income.
  • Credit Score: While you can secure a primary mortgage with lower credit metrics, HELOC approvals typically require a stronger credit history. Scoring above 720 generally unlocks the best variable rate tiers.
Your home value estimate matters

An inflated home value estimate gives you an artificially generous borrowing figure. Base your numbers on a recent appraisal or a conservative estimate, and remember your lender will order their own independent appraisal during underwriting.

Conclusion

Before applying for a home equity line, look at recent comparable sales in your area to establish an honest valuation baseline, calculate your 80% cap, and check your credit standing. Knowing your numbers ahead of time gives you a real advantage when negotiating loan terms with potential lenders.
