Using a HELOC to Pay Off High-Interest Credit Cards

5 min read Mike Lucas

With credit card interest rates hovering near historic highs, managing revolving debt can feel like running on a financial treadmill. For homeowners sitting on substantial property equity, a tempting escape route often emerges: using a Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) to wipe out credit card balances entirely.

On paper, the math looks like an instant win. Swapping a 22% or 25% variable credit card APR for a HELOC rate in the 7–9% range can save thousands of dollars a year in interest alone. But this move transforms unsecured debt into secured debt, a shift that carries a hidden risk you need to understand before moving forward.

The Rewards: Why the Math Works

The primary benefit of using a HELOC for debt consolidation is immediate financial breathing room. Because HELOC interest rates are significantly lower than standard credit cards, more of your monthly payment goes toward reducing your actual principal balance rather than disappearing into compounding interest.

During the initial draw period of a HELOC (typically 10 years), your required minimum payment is usually interest-only. If you use this phase aggressively, paying more than the minimum, you can extinguish your principal balance faster and on your own terms. Our HELOC Payment Calculator shows exactly what interest-only payments would look like on your balance.

The Hidden Danger: Shifting the Risk

Here's the most critical concept to grasp: credit card debt is unsecured debt. If you fall behind on credit card payments, your credit score suffers heavily, but the credit card company can't simply seize your home without extensive legal action.

A HELOC is secured debt, backed directly by your home. The moment you transfer credit card balances onto a HELOC, you're tying your home directly to that debt. If a job loss or medical emergency prevents you from keeping up with HELOC payments down the road, the lender has the legal right to pursue foreclosure.

The Golden Rule of HELOC Consolidation

Consolidation only works if you address the root cause of the debt. If you clear your credit cards with a HELOC but don't change the spending habits that created the debt, you risk running the balances up a second time, leaving you with both a maxed-out credit card lifestyle and a new home equity payment.

How to Consolidate Safely

To execute this strategy successfully, follow this disciplined roadmap:

  1. Freeze the cards. Once your credit cards are paid to zero by the HELOC, remove them from digital wallets to prevent casual overspending.
  2. Pay above the minimum. Don't coast on interest-only payments. Use the money you're saving on interest to actively pay down the HELOC principal each month.
  3. Monitor the variable rate. HELOCs carry variable interest rates. Keep an eye on market conditions so you can budget appropriately if the rate adjusts upward — our HELOC vs. Home Equity Loan comparison can help you weigh a fixed-rate alternative if payment certainty matters more to you.

Is It Right for You?

A HELOC consolidation makes the most sense for homeowners with stable income, a clear plan to avoid re-accumulating high-interest debt, and enough home equity to make the move worthwhile. If any of those pieces are missing, it's worth speaking with a licensed financial professional before converting unsecured debt into a loan secured by your home.
