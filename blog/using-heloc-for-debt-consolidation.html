Debt Consolidation

Using a HELOC to Pay Off High-Interest Credit Cards

Published: June 2026 | Written by HELOC Edge Editorial Team

With credit card interest rates hovering at historic highs, managing revolving debt can feel like running on a financial treadmill. For homeowners sitting on substantial property equity, a tempting escape route often emerges: using a Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) to wipe out credit card balances entirely.

On paper, the math looks like an instant victory. Swapping a 22% or 25% variable credit card APR for a 7.5% or 8% HELOC rate can save you thousands of dollars a year in interest alone. However, this strategic maneuver transforms unsecured debt into secured debt—a shift that comes with a massive hidden risk you must understand before moving forward.

The Rewards: Why the Math Works

The primary benefit of using a HELOC for debt consolidation is immediate financial breathing room. Because HELOC interest rates are significantly lower than standard credit cards, more of your monthly payment goes toward reducing your actual principal balance rather than burning away on compounding interest charges.

Furthermore, during the initial 10-year Draw Period of a HELOC, your required minimum payment is typically interest-only. If you use this phase aggressively—paying far more than the minimum interest requirement—you can rapidly extinguish your principal balance on your own timeline.

The Hidden Danger: Shifting the Risk

Here is the most critical concept to grasp: Credit card debt is **unsecured debt**. If you experience a severe financial crisis and fall behind on credit card payments, your credit score will suffer heavily, but the credit card company cannot step in and seize your personal property without extensive legal actions.

A HELOC is **secured debt** backed directly by your home. The moment you transfer your credit card balance over to a HELOC, you are tying your family's shelter directly to that debt. If unexpected job loss or medical emergencies prevent you from making your HELOC payments down the road, the lender has the legal right to initiate foreclosure proceedings on your house.

The Golden Rule of HELOC Consolidation

Consolidation only works if you treat the root cause of the debt. If you use a HELOC to clear your credit cards, but do not change the spending habits that created the debt in the first place, you risk running up your credit card balances a second time. This leaves you carrying both a maxed-out credit card lifestyle AND a new monthly home equity payment.

How to Consolidate Safely

To execute this financial strategy successfully, follow this disciplined roadmap:

  1. Freeze the Cards: Once your credit cards are paid to zero by the HELOC, remove them from digital wallets to prevent casual overspending.
  2. Pay Above the Minimum: Do not coast on interest-only payments. Use the money you are saving on interest to actively pay down the HELOC principal balance every month.
  3. Monitor Variable Interest Rates: Remember that HELOCs feature variable interest rates. Keep an eye on market conditions so you can budget appropriately if the prime rate adjusts upward.
