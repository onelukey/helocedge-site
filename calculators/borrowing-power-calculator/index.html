How Lenders Calculate Your Borrowing Power

Your HELOC borrowing power isn't based on your home's value alone — it's based on your available equity after subtracting everything you already owe against the property. Lenders express this as a ratio called Combined Loan-to-Value, or CLTV.

The Formula

Lenders typically use this calculation:

(Home Value × Maximum LTV%) − Existing Mortgage Balance − Other Liens = Available HELOC Credit Line

For example, on a $450,000 home with an 85% LTV limit and a $220,000 mortgage balance, the maximum combined debt allowed is $382,500. Subtracting the existing mortgage leaves $162,500 in potential HELOC credit.

Why Lenders Set an LTV Limit

The LTV limit protects the lender if home values fall. By capping combined debt below 100% of the home's value, lenders maintain a buffer of equity that reduces their risk if you're unable to make payments. Limits commonly range from 80% to 90%, depending on the lender, your credit score, and whether the loan is for a primary residence or investment property.

Important

This calculator estimates your maximum theoretical credit line. Actual approval also depends on your credit score, debt-to-income ratio, income verification, and the specific lender's underwriting criteria. Always confirm your exact figure directly with a lender.

What Counts as "Other Liens"?

If you already have a second mortgage, an existing HELOC, or any other loan secured against your home, lenders include this in the CLTV calculation. This reduces your available borrowing power even if your primary mortgage balance is low.
