What this means

What Is LTV and Why It Matters

Loan-to-Value (LTV) is the percentage of your home's value that's covered by debt. It's calculated as: Mortgage Balance ÷ Home Value × 100. Lenders use this figure to assess risk — the lower your LTV, the more equity cushion you have, and the more confident a lender is in approving you for additional borrowing like a HELOC.

LTV vs. CLTV — What's the Difference?

LTV considers only your primary mortgage. CLTV (Combined Loan-to-Value) adds in any other debt secured against your home — a second mortgage, an existing HELOC, or any other lien — divided by your home's value. If you have any additional liens, lenders will use your CLTV, not your plain LTV, to assess HELOC eligibility.

What Counts as a "Good" LTV?

  • Below 60%: Excellent — strong equity position, most lenders will approve favorable terms
  • 60–80%: Good — within typical lender comfort zone for most HELOC products
  • 80–90%: Borderline — some lenders approve here, often with a higher rate or stricter requirements
  • Above 90%: Difficult — few lenders will extend a HELOC at this level
Your home value estimate matters

Using an inflated home value will give you an artificially low LTV. For accuracy, base your figure on a recent appraisal or a conservative estimate from a reputable home value tool, not just what you hope your home is worth.
